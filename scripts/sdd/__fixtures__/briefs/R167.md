## Requirements (SPEC.md)

- **FR-GLOBE-2** *(V212-1, V212-3)* **The sphere from outside.** The globe is the celestial sphere under an orthographic projection seen from outside: radius `GLOBE_RADIUS_SHARE` of the box's shorter side times the zoom, centred in the box; a direction on the near side is drawn, one on the far side is not. **Figures are mirrored**, as on a brass globe: east is to the left of north as seen from the ground and to the right on the globe, and nothing flips them back; the labels' text reads normally. The disc is filled by FR-GLOBE-13's surface; over it, in this order: the equatorial grid every `GLOBE_GRID_STEP_DEG` as hairlines in `--rule`; the ecliptic dashed in `--chart-sun`; the Milky Way's three outlines (FR-LOOK-3's `milkyWay.json`) dotted in `--chart-haze`; the figures as 1 px lines in `--chart-figure`; the stars and the bodies (FR-GLOBE-4); the names (FR-GLOBE-11). The two celestial poles are marked `+` and labelled `NCP` / `SCP` (`PCN` / `PCS`) where they are on the near side. *Verification:* `globe/projection.test.ts` — a direction at the view's centre lands at the box's centre, one 90° off on the rim, one behind is not drawn; the mirror's sign (Betelgeuse right of Rigel's meridian on the globe when facing Orion from outside); a component test counts the grid, the ecliptic, the Milky Way and the figures' paths.

- **FR-GLOBE-3** *(V212-1)* **The horizon and the ground.** The shown place's horizon is a great circle in `--chart-horizon`, its near half drawn; the near disc's part below it is shaded in `--chart-ground` at `GLOBE_GROUND_OPACITY`, and every star, figure, name and body there is drawn at `GLOBE_DIM_OPACITY`, never removed. The zenith is a `+` in `--chart-compass` labelled `zenith` / `cenit`; N, E, S, W stand on the horizon at their points where those are on the near side (FR-I18N-4's abbreviations, the same in both languages), drawn in `--chart-compass`. *Verification:* `globe/scene.test.ts` — Buenos Aires at a fixed instant: the zenith's right ascension is the local sidereal time and its declination the latitude to 0.1°; London: the south celestial pole is on the ground side; Quito: the horizon passes within 1° of both poles; a component test asserts the dimmed class on a star below the horizon and that it is in the DOM.

- **FR-GLOBE-5** *(V212-4)* **Turning with you, or with the stars.** The globe opens **turning with you**: its frame's pole is the shown place's zenith and its longitude zero the north point, the view's centre is held in that frame (at `GLOBE_OPEN_ALT_DEG` toward the visible pole; north on the equator), so the zenith stays up and the sky wheels about the pole as the globe's time runs — the motion seen outside. A `Turn` / `Giro` row in the rail, `[x] with you [ ] with the stars` / `[x] con el horizonte [ ] con las estrellas` (a `ToggleGroup`, FR-I18N-3's neutral Spanish), switches to **turning with the stars**: the frame is the equatorial one (the north celestial pole up), the view's centre is held in right ascension and declination, the stars stand still and the horizon sweeps across them as time runs — the clearest picture of why the seasons change the sky. A switch keeps the direction at the view's centre, so nothing jumps; the choice is the visit's and not saved. *Verification:* `globe/projection.test.ts` — a switch keeps the centre direction to 1e-9; with you, the zenith's screen position does not move across an hour; with the stars, a star's does not; a component test of the row in both languages.

- **FR-GLOBE-6** *(V212-7)* **The hand and the keys.** A pointer drag turns the globe so the point under the pointer stays under it (to the rim's tenth; the view's latitude in its frame clamped to ±90°, so the globe never turns over its pole); the wheel, and a pinch on a touch screen, zoom about the box's centre by `GLOBE_ZOOM_STEP` per notch between `GLOBE_ZOOM_MIN` and `GLOBE_ZOOM_MAX`. The box is focusable (`tabindex="0"`, the focus ring of FR-DS-4's controls); while it has focus `←`/`→` turn the view's longitude by `GLOBE_KEY_STEP_DEG`, `↑`/`↓` its latitude, `+` (and `=`) and `-` zoom one step, and `Home` returns to the opening view at zoom 1 without touching the time or the place; none of them is a page shortcut (FR-DESK-4's list is unchanged). A drag or a key is one write per animation frame with the pointer captured, positions written by ref outside React's render (D-744, D-751), as the window draws. *Verification:* `globe/projection.test.ts` — the grabbed point after a drag is within 0.5 px of the pointer for a drag ending inside 0.8 of the radius; `globe.spec.ts` — keys and wheel change the view, `Home` restores it. *(amended v2.12, V212-12)* Two zoom buttons, `[ + ]` and `[ − ]` (TextButton, the app's bracketed control; accessible names "Zoom in" / "Acercar" and "Zoom out" / "Alejar"), stand at the right end of the readout row under the box (FR-GLOBE-11), since the window has no zoom control to copy and the row is the globe's own: each press is one `GLOBE_ZOOM_STEP` about the box's centre, clamped to `GLOBE_ZOOM_MIN`..`GLOBE_ZOOM_MAX`, and the button is disabled at its end of the range. They are outside the box and the row's height does not change with them, so they never cover the globe or change its size. *Verification:* `SkyGlobe.test.tsx` — a press changes the radius by one step, each button disabled at its limit, the names in both languages; `globe.spec.ts` presses `[ + ]` and `[ − ]`.

- **FR-GLOBE-13** **The twilight on the globe.** The disc's sky side takes FR-LOOK-2's surface for the Sun's altitude at the shown place and the globe's instant (`--chart-sky-day` above 0°, `--chart-sky-twilight` to −6°, `--chart-sky-deep` below), so a time-lapse across sunset darkens the globe, and the ground side's shade lies over it. Every rung is drawn on both sides at every hour: FR-STAR-13's twilight limit thins what a person can see from one place, and the globe is the whole sphere (OQ-58). *Verification:* `globe/scene.test.ts` — the surface's step at three instants of one evening; a component test asserts the class on the disc.

- **FR-GLOBE-17** **Pure modules, and the physics untouched.** The geometry is pure and tested apart from the DOM: `globe/projection.ts` (unit vectors, the two frames, the view's axes, the projection, a polyline's near-side path, the ground's region, a drag's turn) and `globe/scene.ts` (the shown place's zenith, north and east as J2000 directions at an instant through FR-STAR-2's rotation, alt/az of any direction, the count, the list, the figures' label anchors). - **FR-STAR-2** **Positions, and how right they are.** Every catalogue star's altitude and azimuth at the shown instant come from one rotation per instant from the J2000 equatorial frame to the observer's horizon (`astronomy-engine`'s `Rotation_EQJ_HOR`, precession and nutation inside it), applied to every star, on the main thread in the astronomy chunk's manner (PLAN D-2's path for the Sun, `lib/skyBodies.ts`'s for the Moon): the worker is never called, and the rotation is computed once per shown instant and memoised on it — a change of `t` is a new rotation, a frame under the sensor or the hand is not; one 3 × 3 matrix and 900 products is nothing FR-LIVE-5's once-a-second rule needs to ration, and rationing it would make the stars jump when `t` runs fast (FR-SKY-8). Altitudes are geometric, with no refraction, as the passes' are, so a satellite and the star it passes are in one frame. The planets are evaluated through the same library at the same tick. *Verification:* `tests/fixtures/stars/<site>-<instant>.json`, the owner's capture from a published ephemeris (Stellarium or the USNO, named in the fixture's `.meta.json`, as the Heavens-Above fixtures are; *as captured, V24-12:* IAU SOFA through astropy for the stars and JPL Horizons for the planets) at the three R1 sites at one instant each, for twelve stars across the sky and the five planets; `skyStars.test.ts` holds every one within `STAR_TOLERANCE_DEG` (0.25°, half the Moon's width, five times tighter than FR-GUIDE-4's degree, chosen so a figure's shape is right and not only its place).

- **D-1034 — The globe lives in `skychart/globe/` and is not a `SkyChart` view.** (FR-GLOBE-1, FR-GLOBE-14, FR-GLOBE-15, FR-GLOBE-17; SPEC FR-SKY-1 as amended.) New: `src/ui/components/guide/skychart/globe/{constants,projection,scene}.ts` (pure), `GlobeRenderer.ts` (the SVG built once and written per frame), `SkyGlobe.tsx` and `SkyGlobe.module.css` (the component: the box's measure, the pointer, the wheel and the keys, the renderer's host). `SkyGlobe.tsx` is the default export of a `React.lazy` in `Stars.tsx`, so Rollup names its chunk `SkyGlobe-*.js` and `scripts/bundle-budget.ts` gains the row `{ name: 'globe', match: /^SkyGlobe-.*\.js$/, limitKb: 15 }` (`GLOBE_BUDGET_KB`). The globe draws no pass, takes no `SkyScene` and has its own camera and instant, so it does not go through `SkyChart`'s view switch or `useSkyScene`'s layer table; it reads the catalogue, `starGlyphs.markFor`, `skySurface.tintStep` and `starLabels.ts` from the window's directory as imports. The directory is the `chart` lane's (`skychart/**` except `window/`).

- **D-1036 — The projection is the spike's, without the mirror.** (FR-GLOBE-2, FR-GLOBE-5, FR-GLOBE-6.) `globe/projection.ts` ports `globe.ts`: `Vec3` helpers, `radec`, `Frame` (`EQUATORIAL`; `localFrame(zenith, north)` for turning with you), `viewAxes(frame, lon, lat)`, `project(p, s)` → `[x, y, depth]` with no x flip (the true outside view, V212-3), `pathOf` (a polyline's near-side path), `groundPath`, `greatCircle`, `parallel`, `meridian`, `ECLIPTIC_POLE`; and two the spike had in `main.tsx`: `coordsIn(to, from, lon, lat)` (a frame switch that keeps the centre) and `dragTurn(p, from, to)` (the view's new longitude and latitude so the grabbed direction lands under the pointer, latitude clamped to ±90°). The opening view is `openView(sky)`: in the local frame, longitude 180° (south) when the place's latitude is negative and 0° (north) otherwise, latitude `GLOBE_OPEN_ALT_DEG`.

- **D-1037 — The scene is one rotation per instant, inverted.** (FR-GLOBE-3, FR-GLOBE-8, FR-GLOBE-12, FR-GLOBE-13, FR-GLOBE-17; SPEC FR-STAR-2.) `globe/scene.ts`'s `globeSky(place, t)` takes the J2000→horizon rotation FR-STAR-2 already builds (`astronomy-engine`'s `Rotation_EQJ_HOR`, through `lib/skyStars.ts`'s path) and reads the zenith, north and east as J2000 directions from its transpose's columns; the bodies come from `lib/skyBodies.ts` at the same instant and are turned back to J2000 by the same transpose, so they sit where the window puts them. `altAz(sky, s)` reads any direction's altitude and azimuth; `upCount(sky, dirs)` and `upList(sky, catalog, bodies, lang)` (the `FIELD_LIST_MAX` brightest above the horizon, `FieldList`'s row shape); `figureAnchor(figure, drawn)` (the drawn vertex nearest the drawn endpoints' centroid, D-1041); `surfaceAt(sky)` is `skySurface.tintStep` of the Sun's altitude. The positions of the 904 bright stars as unit vectors are built once per catalogue, and the deep catalogue's on load.

- **D-1041 — `starLabels.ts` gains two options, off by default.** (FR-GLOBE-11.) `placeLabels(input, options?)` takes `fixed?: readonly FixedLabel[]` — the bodies' labels, placed after the compass at the first free of right, left, above and below, and never dropped — and `figureStart?: 'centroid' | 'vertex'`, `'centroid'` by default; with `'vertex'` the walk's first place is `figureAnchor`'s vertex. The window and the polar chart pass neither, so their placement and snapshots do not move; `starLabels.test.ts` gains the globe's cases. *(R168, as built)* The vertex reaches the placer as `FigureLabel.anchor` (optional; a figure without one keeps the centroid under `'vertex'`, and the grid stays over the drawn endpoints' extent, nearest the start). A fixed label with no free place takes the first of the four inside the box and clear of the labels already placed, else the first inside, else the right, and the named mark's label falls back the same way, so neither writes over a body's name; it is `kind: 'fixed'`; a star name or the landmark's on a fixed label's mark is not drawn twice; a fixed label gives way to the tap's label on its own mark (the tap's, placed next, carries the magnitude); and a figure never removes one to keep its name, as it removes a star's.

- **D-1044 — A drag turns about the frame's pole and reaches what the pole lets it.** (FR-GLOBE-6; R167.) `dragTurn(p, from, to)` solves the turn in closed form: in the view's own terms a direction at frame latitude φ has screen x = cos φ·sin(λ − lon) whatever the view's latitude, so x fixes the longitude (two roots, the one whose view axes — centre and up — turn least from the present view's chosen, so where the nearer root is clamped the point lags the hand rather than the globe flipping to the other root half a turn away) and the latitude is the turn about screen right taking the grabbed direction's (depth, y) to the pointer's. With the pole kept up, a target further than cos φ radii from the screen's vertical is unreachable; there the grabbed point goes to the nearest x it can reach, and `projection.test.ts` holds the 0.5 px rule on the reachable drags (over 250 of 400 seeded drags inside 0.8 of the radius). `unproject(p, x, y)` (a screen point to its near-side direction, off-disc points taken to the rim) is the drag's other half and is exported for the renderer's hover. `viewAxes` drops the spike's ±89.5° clamp: up is written as −sin(lat)·h + cos(lat)·pole, exact at the poles, so the ±90° clamp is the drag's alone.

- **D-1045 — The globe's planets are `skyStars.ts`'s calls, not `starsAt`.** (FR-GLOBE-17, FR-STAR-2; R167.) `globeSky(place, t)` makes `Rotation_EQJ_HOR` itself and evaluates the five planets with the same `Equator(…, true, true)`, `Horizon` and `Illumination` calls `lib/skyStars.ts` makes, rather than calling `starsAt`: `starsAt` memoises one slot keyed on the catalogue, so the globe asking it with an empty catalogue would evict the stars page's entry, and asking it with the page's catalogue would do 1 033 products the globe already has. `scene.test.ts` holds every star's altitude and azimuth and every planet's to `starsAt`'s at three sites. `globeSky` keeps its own one-slot memo on `(t, lat, lon, altM)`. `BODIES_EVERY_MS` is `lib/playback.ts`'s, re-exported by `globe/constants.ts` rather than restated. `upList(sky, catalog, planets, lang)` takes the planets as an argument so the planets layer off passes `[]`; a planet's row index is past the catalogue's length (D-750). `figureAnchor(figure, drawn)` returns `{ index, x, y }` (the vertex's star index and its screen point) or `null`, counting a segment only when both its ends are drawn. The zenith's test turns the J2000 zenith to the equator of date before comparing it with the sidereal time: J2000 and of-date right ascensions differ by about 0.3° in 2026, three times the test's tolerance.

- **D-89 — The turn cap is 250, and a headless session commits as it goes.** R17, the language task, ended at turn 121 of 120 with the `src/i18n` catalogs written, some thirty files rewired through them, the co-located tests not yet migrated to the new signatures, and **nothing committed** — so the driver's step-4 check (has the branch any commits?) found an empty branch and scored the whole run lost.
