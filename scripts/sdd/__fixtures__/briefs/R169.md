## Requirements (SPEC.md)

- **FR-GLOBE-2** *(V212-1, V212-3)* **The sphere from outside.** The globe is the celestial sphere under an orthographic projection seen from outside: radius `GLOBE_RADIUS_SHARE` of the box's shorter side times the zoom, centred in the box; a direction on the near side is drawn, one on the far side is not. **Figures are mirrored**, as on a brass globe: east is to the left of north as seen from the ground and to the right on the globe, and nothing flips them back; the labels' text reads normally. The disc is filled by FR-GLOBE-13's surface; over it, in this order: the equatorial grid every `GLOBE_GRID_STEP_DEG` as hairlines in `--rule`; the ecliptic dashed in `--chart-sun`; the Milky Way's three outlines (FR-LOOK-3's `milkyWay.json`) dotted in `--chart-haze`; the figures as 1 px lines in `--chart-figure`; the stars and the bodies (FR-GLOBE-4); the names (FR-GLOBE-11). The two celestial poles are marked `+` and labelled `NCP` / `SCP` (`PCN` / `PCS`) where they are on the near side. *Verification:* `globe/projection.test.ts` — a direction at the view's centre lands at the box's centre, one 90° off on the rim, one behind is not drawn; the mirror's sign (Betelgeuse right of Rigel's meridian on the globe when facing Orion from outside); a component test counts the grid, the ecliptic, the Milky Way and the figures' paths.

- **FR-GLOBE-3** *(V212-1)* **The horizon and the ground.** The shown place's horizon is a great circle in `--chart-horizon`, its near half drawn; the near disc's part below it is shaded in `--chart-ground` at `GLOBE_GROUND_OPACITY`, and every star, figure, name and body there is drawn at `GLOBE_DIM_OPACITY`, never removed. The zenith is a `+` in `--chart-compass` labelled `zenith` / `cenit`; N, E, S, W stand on the horizon at their points where those are on the near side (FR-I18N-4's abbreviations, the same in both languages), drawn in `--chart-compass`. *Verification:* `globe/scene.test.ts` — Buenos Aires at a fixed instant: the zenith's right ascension is the local sidereal time and its declination the latitude to 0.1°; London: the south celestial pole is on the ground side; Quito: the horizon passes within 1° of both poles; a component test asserts the dimmed class on a star below the horizon and that it is in the DOM.

- **FR-GLOBE-4** *(V212-1)* **The marks.** Stars by FR-LOOK-4's ladder and FR-LOOK-5's tones (`starGlyphs.markFor`, the window's): a `*` character at its size to `STAR_DOT_MAG`, a round dot below, sized by `DOT_RADIUS_PX`. The dots are not nodes: every dot of one tone, one size step and one side of the horizon is a zero-length round-capped segment in one `<path>`, so the node count is fixed at mount and never above `GLOBE_MAX_NODES`. The Sun as the window's Sun mark in `--chart-sun`, the Moon as FR-LOOK-7's disc with its phase, the planets as `@` (FR-LOOK-4), placed from `lib/skyBodies.ts` for the shown place and the globe's instant. No bloom, no horizon band and no haze fill: the globe is the atlas, the window the view. *Verification:* `globe/renderer.test.ts` — the node count with the faint stars off and on is the same and ≤ `GLOBE_MAX_NODES`; a dot path's segment count equals its bucket's stars on the near side.

- **FR-GLOBE-5** *(V212-4)* **Turning with you, or with the stars.** The globe opens **turning with you**: its frame's pole is the shown place's zenith and its longitude zero the north point, the view's centre is held in that frame (at `GLOBE_OPEN_ALT_DEG` toward the visible pole; north on the equator), so the zenith stays up and the sky wheels about the pole as the globe's time runs — the motion seen outside. A `Turn` / `Giro` row in the rail, `[x] with you [ ] with the stars` / `[x] con el horizonte [ ] con las estrellas` (a `ToggleGroup`, FR-I18N-3's neutral Spanish), switches to **turning with the stars**: the frame is the equatorial one (the north celestial pole up), the view's centre is held in right ascension and declination, the stars stand still and the horizon sweeps across them as time runs — the clearest picture of why the seasons change the sky. A switch keeps the direction at the view's centre, so nothing jumps; the choice is the visit's and not saved. *Verification:* `globe/projection.test.ts` — a switch keeps the centre direction to 1e-9; with you, the zenith's screen position does not move across an hour; with the stars, a star's does not; a component test of the row in both languages.

## Decisions (PLAN.md)

- **D-744 — The ref path, and V24-9 held.** (FR-STAR-9; V24-9.) The field's nodes are rendered once and each frame writes `x`, `y` and `visibility` to them outside React, as `spike/stars/Field.tsx`'s `RefField` does. Through React the field falls to 19 draws/s at 4× on the Pixel 5 profile; by ref it holds about 100 at 1×, 4× and 6×. On the owner's iPhone both paths draw at the sensor's 61 events/s. Glyphs cost what discs cost, so the stars stay characters.

- **D-751 — One rotation channel; the star field reads it by ref.** (FR-STAR-9 as amended; D-744.) `useViewRotation` publishes each new rotation twice in the same animation frame: to React state, as today, for the window's existing layers, the readout, the ground state and the gutter (about twenty nodes, which React redraws within budget), and to a subscriber list. `skychart/window/StarField.tsx` is a `memo` child that renders its nodes once per catalogue and layer set — one `<text>` per star and planet, one `<line>` per segment, one `<text>` per name — and on each published rotation projects and writes `x`, `y`, `dx`, `dy` and `visibility` to them directly. Both writes land before the frame's paint, so the horizon and the stars never disagree. The label placement (D-755) runs only when the rotation has moved past `converged()` since the last placement, so names do not re-collide at the sensor's jitter.

- **D-817 — The drag rate is the best of three drags, and the constant is the middle of three runs.** (FR-SKY-5, FR-GUIDE-6; D-62, D-793; R124.) On one build and one page, three 5 s drags under the 6× throttle read 39.4, 46.5 and 56.6/s — a swing twice the tenth FR-SKY-5 allows — and every swing is downward: another process taking the core slows the page, and nothing speeds it up. So `dome-perf.spec.ts` drags three times on the same page and reports the best drag as the rate the page can do; a median would have failed an honest build one run in three (46.5 against a floor of 52.0), where the best of three varied within 1/s across runs (59.8, 59.4, 58.8). The committed constant is the middle of three such runs on `ece8bfd` (390 px 59.4, 1280 px 53.1, Apple silicon, 2026-10-26); the floor is nine tenths of the phone's and never under 30, and the desktop's is reported, not gated (D-91). The log line carries the line layer's braille dot count, so a run says whether the stars were on (1132 without, 1154 with the twenty and five). The constant is one machine's, as D-62's numbers are, and the spec stays a measurement run with `DOME_PERF=1`, never a CI gate; on another machine the runner measures `ece8bfd` by the same spec and hands the phone's rate in as `DOME_PERF_ECE8BFD`, which replaces the constant for that run.

- **D-1034 — The globe lives in `skychart/globe/` and is not a `SkyChart` view.** (FR-GLOBE-1, FR-GLOBE-14, FR-GLOBE-15, FR-GLOBE-17; SPEC FR-SKY-1 as amended.) New: `src/ui/components/guide/skychart/globe/{constants,projection,scene}.ts` (pure), `GlobeRenderer.ts` (the SVG built once and written per frame), `SkyGlobe.tsx` and `SkyGlobe.module.css` (the component: the box's measure, the pointer, the wheel and the keys, the renderer's host). `SkyGlobe.tsx` is the default export of a `React.lazy` in `Stars.tsx`, so Rollup names its chunk `SkyGlobe-*.js` and `scripts/bundle-budget.ts` gains the row `{ name: 'globe', match: /^SkyGlobe-.*\.js$/, limitKb: 15 }` (`GLOBE_BUDGET_KB`). The globe draws no pass, takes no `SkyScene` and has its own camera and instant, so it does not go through `SkyChart`'s view switch or `useSkyScene`'s layer table; it reads the catalogue, `starGlyphs.markFor`, `skySurface.tintStep` and `starLabels.ts` from the window's directory as imports. The directory is the `chart` lane's (`skychart/**` except `window/`).

- **D-1038 — The renderer: fixed nodes, bucketed dots, frames only when something moves.** (FR-GLOBE-4, FR-GLOBE-10, FR-GLOBE-14, FR-GLOBE-15.) `GlobeRenderer` creates at mount: the disc and the ground, the grid, the ecliptic and the Milky Way paths, one path per figure layer side (above and below the horizon), one `<text>` per star of V ≤ `STAR_DOT_MAG` (about 93) and per named star and figure (about 23 and 89), the Sun, the Moon (FR-LOOK-7's `MoonDisc` geometry), the planets, the compass, the zenith and the poles, and 40 dot paths (4 tones × 5 sizes × 2 sides) — the spike's 324 nodes, under `GLOBE_MAX_NODES`. `set(state)` merges a change and schedules one `requestAnimationFrame`; a frame projects every direction, writes the `d`, `x`, `y`, `visibility` and class attributes by ref, and places the labels (D-1041). There is no idle loop: a frame runs on a drag, a wheel, a key, a prop change and each playback tick. The deep stars are appended to the dot buckets' arrays when `deepChunk.ts` resolves; no node is created. Colours are classes in `SkyGlobe.module.css` over the tokens FR-GLOBE-14 names; the opacities are `globe/constants.ts`'s numbers written as attributes (FR-DS-2 admits a unitless opacity).

- **D-1047 — The renderer fills React's `<svg>`, and what it draws that D-1038 left open.** (FR-GLOBE-2, FR-GLOBE-4, FR-GLOBE-10, FR-GLOBE-11, FR-GLOBE-16, FR-X-3; R169.) `SkyGlobe` renders one empty `<svg>` (the class, `role="img"` and `data-globe` React's) and `GlobeRenderer` takes it, makes every child with `createElementNS` in the root's own `namespaceURI` and writes the label and the `viewBox`; `dispose` empties it and leaves the root. - **D-1048 — The hand is a ref, and what it owns.** (FR-GLOBE-5, FR-GLOBE-6, FR-GLOBE-9, FR-GLOBE-10, FR-GLOBE-11; D-744, D-751, D-1040; R169.) The view (`lon`, `lat`, `zoom`) is a ref in `SkyGlobe`; a drag, a wheel, a pinch and a key write it and call `renderer.set`, which draws on its own animation frame; React renders the component only for a prop, the named object or the dragging flag. A drag folds the moves since the last frame into one `dragTurn` on the next frame, from the last point applied to the latest, against `projectorFor` of the view as it now stands (not the last drawn frame's), so a late frame never turns twice. A press that travels under `HIT_RADIUS_PX` holds the globe still and names on release what was under the press; past it, it is a drag and names nothing.
